# SAI: Talking Points for Our Meeting

## 1. My vision and commitment

- The Director mode wireframe is **one feature**, not the whole idea. The bigger vision is a platform where children **draw, write and direct their own animated films**.
- I'm very interested in this project and want to make it **technically strong**:
  - our own animation engine and language (the SAI Movie API), not just a wrapper around someone else's AI
  - safety by design, so every AI output is checked before a child sees it
  - low cost per child, running on ordinary school laptops
  - AI providers can be swapped, so we're not locked into one company
- **What I'd build first:** SAI Canvas, then Director mode, working towards a 5-minute film made over a term.
- **After that:** many more ideas that combine STEM and values education (see section 4).

## 2. How I can help with the business plan and roadmap

- **Own the technology and product sections:** the roadmap, what makes us innovative, the technical side of safeguarding and responsible AI, running costs and how we scale.
- **Co-write the overall plan with you**, so it reads as one shared vision.
- **Build demos and wireframes** for funders, schools and partners.
- **Turn pilot feedback into features**, adjusting the product after each club session.
- **Provide built-in progress evidence** from children's work to support impact reports.
- **Formalise my role**, e.g. Co-founder and Technical Director.

## 3. Questions for you: are we aligned?

- What's your vision for year one: the club only, or the club plus our own platform?
- Which funders are you targeting, and what do they want to see on the technology side?
- Pilot: when does it start, how many children, what ages, and what devices does the school have?
- How long are sessions and terms? (A film made over a term fits well.)
- Which values do we start with, and is there a curriculum you have in mind?
- Is the girls' confidence strand a separate group, or part of every session?
- How do we split roles and decisions, and how do we formalise them (titles, shareholding)?

## 4. What's possible when values and STEM are combined

| Idea                             | The value                   | The STEM idea               | How it works                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Choice films**                 | Choices have consequences   | _If/else_ logic (branching) | The film pauses at a moral moment: _"Share the lantern, or keep it?"_ Each choice leads to a different scene. The child builds both paths, so they're writing branching logic.                              |
| **Character traits as settings** | Courage, kindness, patience | _Variables_                 | A character has trait sliders. Turn courage up and they stride into the storm; turn it down and they hesitate. The child learns that a setting changes behaviour, and that traits shape actions.            |
| **Fix the film**                 | Resilience, perseverance    | _Debugging_                 | Sometimes a scene doesn't do what the child meant. Fixing it is framed as "getting it right takes a few tries", which builds a growth mindset through real problem-solving.                                 |
| **The other side of the story**  | Empathy                     | _Same data, different view_ | One click retells the film from another character's point of view (the "villain", the bystander). Children see the same events through someone else's eyes.                                                 |
| **Did the AI get it right?**     | Honesty, critical thinking  | _AI literacy_               | The child compares what they meant with what the AI produced, and corrects it. This teaches that AI can be wrong and the child is the one in charge. It's responsible AI awareness built into the activity. |
| **Real-world science scenes**    | Curiosity                   | _Physics and nature_        | Day turning to night, rain, light, gravity. A "science mode" where the sky really follows the sun, and objects really fall and float.                                                                       |
| **Story to game**                | Fairness, teamwork          | _Game design_               | The same engine turns a film into a simple playable game, where you guide the hero to help someone. Children go from watching to designing.                                                                 |
| **Class Cinema**                 | Teamwork, responsibility    | _Collaborative projects_    | Each child makes one chapter of a shared film. The credits give everyone a role: Director, Engineer, Artist, Writer.                                                                                        |
| **Behind the curtain**           | Confidence in technology    | _Real code_                 | The child sees and edits the actual instructions behind their film, e.g. `hero.hop()`. This is where girls see themselves as engineers, not just users.                                                     |
| **Voice and language**           | Inclusion                   | _Speech technology_         | Children who find writing hard can speak their story instead. Narration can also be in a family's home language.                                                                                            |

**Suggested core:** Choice films, Fix the film, Did the AI get it right? and Behind the curtain. Together they take a child from storytelling, to logic, to AI literacy, to real code.
